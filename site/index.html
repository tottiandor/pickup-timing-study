So nobody has an edge from reading it. We measured what managers actually do once the lineups are out — and what little separates a good pickup from a bad one turns out to have nothing to do with being quick.

Two things this study is not going to tell you. That pickups spike when the lineups drop at KO−75 — you already knew, because you are one of the people doing it. And that sniped players start almost every time: that is not a finding, it is the definition. You make the pickup because you have seen he is starting. Both are in the timing and method pages as background. Everything below is what is left once you take them out.

The only decision left

By the time you are in the window, "is he playing" has been answered for free, for everyone. At that moment there are on average — confirmed starters sitting unowned. Which of them you take is the entire skill.

Managers land at the — percentile of the confirmed starters in front of them — worth — FPts over flipping a coin between them. That is the sum total of demonstrated selection skill in the most-watched five minutes of the fantasy week.

Position, once you divide out scarcity

The obvious cut is by position, and the obvious reading of it is wrong. Waiver forwards start half as often as waiver defenders — but that is almost entirely the pool. Only — of the free forwards in a given gameweek went on to start at all, against — of free defenders. Real teams field four defenders and one or two forwards, so the spare forwards lying around are overwhelmingly bench players.

Start rate, before and after the pool

the manager's rate against the base rate they drew from

Raw, the gap between defenders and forwards is 16 points of start rate. Measured as lift over each position's own free pool it is under six. Roughly three quarters of the apparent position effect on start rate was scarcity, not skill.

What does survive is points. These are all confirmed starters, so the pool cannot explain it — a starting defender simply returns more in this scoring system than a starting midfielder.

What each lever is worth

FPts per pickup

Always taking the defender is worth more than every bit of selection skill managers currently show, and roughly three times as much as winning the race. Both levers are available to anyone looking at the same screen.

Buy early for quality, late for certainty

The trade is real and it runs the way round most people assume it doesn't. A waiver claim days ahead is a bet on a better footballer who might be benched. The informed pickup is a worse footballer you know will play.

The waiver market cannot price a week

This one is not obvious at all. Above about $9 a FAAB bid starts losing you claims, and at no price does it buy points.

A big bid is a signal you are in a fight, and most people in a fight lose it. The money, in full.

What eighteen divisions know

Each division is a separate market. When several of them independently chase the same player in the same gameweek, he tends to deliver — the one genuine crowd signal in the data, and one a single league cannot produce.
