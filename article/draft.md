# The Fantrax waiver wire is a timing game. Just not the one you think.

*By Tottiandor*

Seventy-five minutes before kickoff, the team sheets land and a few thousand
of us do the same thing at the same time. I pulled every transaction from two
25/26 Fantrax leagues to find out whether it works: the International Genie
League, 144 teams across twelve divisions, and the DraftPL Community League,
72 teams across six. That is 18 separate waiver markets and **22,488
pickups**.

Here is what I thought I'd find, and here is what was waiting instead.

## The bit everyone already knows

Adds spike when the lineups drop. In the five minutes after the team sheets
are published there were 1,241 pickups across the two leagues. In the five
minutes before, 126. Call it ten times the traffic the moment the news lands.

Lovely chart. Tells you nothing.

You knew that already, because you are one of the people doing it. And the
companion stat, that sniped players start 97.7% of the time against 66.7% for
a waiver claim, is circular. You make the pickup *because* you have seen he is
starting. That is the definition of the act, not a benefit of it.

So park both. The interesting question is what happens after everyone has the
same information.

## The only decision left

By the time you are in that window, "is he playing" has been answered. For
free. For everybody.

And the shelf is not bare. At the moment of a typical informed pickup there
were **53 confirmed starters** sitting unowned in that division. Fifty-three.
You are not fighting over scraps, you are standing in front of a wall of
players who are all definitely playing.

Which one you take is the whole skill. So how did we do?

We landed on the **52nd percentile** of those confirmed starters. Worth
**+0.72 FPts** over shutting your eyes and picking one at random.

That is it. That is the sum total of demonstrated selection skill in the
most-watched five minutes of the fantasy week.

I want to be fair here, because there is a lazy version of this stat doing
the rounds in my own head. The best available starter each week averaged
22.98 points against the 7.31 we took, and it is tempting to call that
fifteen points left on the table. It isn't. Nobody picks the week's
23-pointer every Saturday, and the starter pool is wildly lopsided: median
6.50, but p90 of 16.75 and p99 of 28.75. Most of that upside is variance
nobody forecasts. The coin flip is the honest comparison, and we beat it by
less than a point.

## Buying early gets you the better footballer

This one surprised me.

Strip out the certainty and compare like with like, so only the pickups where
the player started:

| how you got him | if he started |
|---|---|
| waivers, before the team sheet | **8.38** |
| sniped, KO-75 to KO-70 | 7.88 |
| late add, inside KO-70 | 7.19 |

The waiver claim you made on a Tuesday, blind, is the better player. Sniping
does not get you quality. It stops you wasting the transaction: the bust rate,
meaning under 2 FPts, nearly halves from 32.5% to 17.4%.

That is a trade, and it is worth knowing which side of it you are on. Buy
early for quality, buy late for certainty.

As for being quickest on the draw once the news is out, that is worth **+0.58
FPts per pickup** (give or take 0.23, so it is a shade over two and a half
standard errors). It exists. It is small. Setting an alarm for it is probably
not the best use of your Saturday morning.

## The position trap

Now the bit where I was wrong, and the correction is more useful than the
original finding.

Waiver forwards start half as often as waiver defenders. Obvious conclusion:
forwards are risky pickups, be careful. Wrong conclusion.

Look at the pool instead of the pickup. Of the forwards sitting free in a
given gameweek, only **4.9%** went on to start. For defenders it is 15.8%.
Premier League sides field four defenders and one or two forwards, so the
spare forwards lying around the waiver wire are overwhelmingly bench players.
The pool is different, not the decision.

Measure each position as lift over its own free pool and the defender-forward
gap collapses from 16 points of start rate to under six. Roughly three
quarters of what looked like a position effect was positional scarcity.

What does survive is points, because here both sides are confirmed starters
and the pool cannot explain it. A starting defender returns **8.79**. A
starting midfielder returns **7.36**. If the hole in your side is a free slot
rather than a forced one, take the defender.

## Now the one that will cost you points

FAAB. This is where the money is, and where I think most leagues are quietly
setting fire to theirs.

**71% of the season's entire FAAB budget gets spent in the first six
gameweeks.**

At the worst exchange rate of the year.

| | GW1-6 | GW33-38 |
|---|---|---|
| mean bid | $10.56 | $0.30 |
| points per dollar | **0.68** | **12.96** |
| claims won | 29.5% | 38.8% |

A dollar in the run-in buys nineteen times what a dollar in August buys, and
wins you more claims while it is at it. The price of a contested player falls
from $8.47 to $0.78 across the season, and the share of contested auctions
won with a **$0 bid** climbs from 39.5% to 77.5%. By spring you are mostly
just asking politely.

Within a single auction the money still works, obviously. Highest bid wins,
that is the mechanism. But look at what happens to your odds as the bid
climbs:

| bid | claims won |
|---|---|
| $0 (priority) | 31.9% |
| $4-9 | **56.2%** |
| $10-24 | 39.3% |
| $25+ | **21.8%** |

Win rate peaks around a fiver and then falls off a cliff. Not because big
bids lose auctions, but because **a big bid is a signal that you are in a
fight**, and most people in a fight lose it. You bid $25 on the player
everyone else is also chasing.

## In defence of the big spender

Here is the objection I had to go back and test, because it is a good one.

An expensive claim is often somebody who will not kick a ball for weeks. An
injury return. A January signing. A kid about to get a run of games. Judging
that bid on the gameweek straight after you made it is daft, because the
whole point was the month after.

The data backs the mechanism. Only **10.5%** of $25+ claims never started at
all for the team that bought them, against **37.1%** of free claims. They
arrive late, on average 1.72 gameweeks after the claim, but they do arrive.
Pay up and you are buying a near-certainty that he eventually turns up.

What you are not buying is a better player.

Points per start is the measure that settles it, because it throws tenure out
entirely. Total points while held rewards owning somebody for a long time
however badly he plays, and expensive claims get held four times longer, so
that number flatters them rotten. PPS asks one thing: when he started, what
did he score?

Free claims, **7.76**. Claims over $10, **6.73**. A gap of 1.03 either side
of 0.22, which is comfortably significant.

Same story across the calendar. PPS barely moves between August and May, 8.43
to 9.09, while the starts a dollar buys rise almost fivefold over the same
stretch. Late money is not finding better players. It is buying the same
players cheaper.

So should you hoard your budget until April? No, and I want to be careful
here, because FAAB is use-it-or-lose-it. Teams got through $89 each on
average, and a budget you die with is worth nothing. In raw points a $10+
claim returned 72.8 while held against 20.7 for a free one.

The advice is not don't spend.

The advice is don't spend in August, when a contested player costs $8.47 and
the same contested player costs 78 cents in the spring.

## One thing the crowd gets right

Last one, and it is only visible because 18 divisions were drafting and
claiming independently of each other.

When several divisions chase the same player in the same gameweek, he
delivers. A player wanted by one division returned 5.17 points. One wanted by
seven to eleven returned **6.86**, and started 72% of the time against 61%.

It flattens at the top, mind. The 12+ group is no better than the 7-11 group.
The very most fashionable pickup of the week is not better than the merely
popular one, so you can stop feeling clever about winning those.

## What I'd do differently on Saturday

Four things, in order of how much they are worth.

**Stop spending in August.** This is the big one, and it is not close.
Nothing else here moves your season as much as refusing to pay $10 for a
player who will cost 30 cents in February.

**When you snipe, take the defender.** Free slot, confirmed starter, defender
over midfielder is worth about a point and a half a go. That is two and a
half times what being first through the door is worth.

**Stop treating the waiver wire as a panic button.** The blind Tuesday claim
gets you the better footballer. Sniping is insurance against a wasted
transaction, which is worth something, but it is not an upgrade.

**And spend thirty more seconds choosing.** Fifty-three confirmed starters
and we pick the median one. The lineups tell you who is playing. They do not
tell you who is any good, and on the evidence we have mostly stopped looking
once the first question is answered.
